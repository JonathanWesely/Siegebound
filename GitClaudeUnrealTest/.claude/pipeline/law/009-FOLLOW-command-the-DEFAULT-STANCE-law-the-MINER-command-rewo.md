Added 2026-08-02 (Jonathan directive, verbatim: *"I want to add another button to control units. This one is 'Follow' — it basically is a command that makes the unit follow around the player. There is only one mouse scroll circle used for this, and it is just the circle used to indicate what units follow; the units do not attack while in this command. Also, I want this to be the default behavior of all units that spawn, except for units that cannot follow commands such as the Ogre."* · follow-up: *"put the follow button on the 'c' key"* · and the miner rework, verbatim: *"we are going to change miner behavior a little bit, they now do receive all the commands, but they function differently for the miners…"*). Batch **FOLLOW-COMMAND**, TASK-395..408. **EXTENDS — does not supersede — "Group orders — 3-zone HOLD + AMBUSH"**: HOLD and AMBUSH keep their 3-stage pick, their zones, their leash rules and their byte-identical set. Follow is a THIRD group type with a ONE-stage pick.

### 1. THE COMMAND — enum, key, and the single-circle pick
- **Enum:** `ESiegeGroupCommandType : uint8 { Hold, Ambush, Follow }` in `UnitCommand.h` — **`Follow` is APPENDED**, so `Hold == 0` / `Ambush == 1` are byte-preserved. `ESiegeUnitCommand { Attack, Hold, Defend }` (the STANCE enum, whose byte layout `WBP_HUD`'s switch pins depend on) is **NOT touched** — Follow is a group order, never a stance.
- **`FSiegeUnitGroup` is UNCHANGED.** A Follow group carries `Type = Follow`, `PositionRadius == AttackRadius == 0`, both centers `ZeroVector`, both marker decals null. Every zone test in `UpdateStateGrouped` is therefore unreachable for a Follow group *because Follow never enters that function* (see §4) — not because a zero radius happens to fail.
- **Input asset:** ONE new **`IA_CmdFollow`** (`/Game/Input/Actions/IA_CmdFollow`, Digital/bool) mapped to **`C`** in the existing `/Game/Input/IMC_Hero`. Controller soft-ref `CmdFollowActionAsset` (`TSoftObjectPtr<UInputAction>`) → resolved `CmdFollowAction` → `ETriggerEvent::Started` → `OnCmdFollowPressed()`. **Null-safe resolve is law** (missing asset ⇒ C inert, logged once, never a crash — the shipped `IA_Cmd*` pattern). ⚠ **`IMC_Hero` is a BINARY `.uasset` the manager cannot text-verify.** Letter keys in use today: **Q** Rally · **T** Attack · **E** Defend · **R** Hold · **F** Ambush (plus Space jump, Shift sprint, Left-Alt UI cursor, 1–6 cards, LMB attack, RMB/Esc cancel). **C reads as FREE** — the C++ side has no `EKeys::C` and no `Crouch` binding anywhere — but the art-director MUST open `IMC_Hero` in-editor and CONFIRM C is unmapped before adding, and **FLAG (never silently stomp)** any conflict.
- **ONE CIRCLE, ONE STAGE (this is the whole difference from Hold/Ambush).** C enters the EXISTING pick machinery at **`EGroupPickStage::Select` only** and CONFIRMS there: mouse-wheel resizes the select circle (`GroupRadiusWheelStep` 100, min 200, max 5000, default `GroupSelectRadiusDefault` **1200** — all reused unchanged), LMB confirms, RMB/Esc cancels. **There is NO position zone and NO attack zone for Follow**, and the pick must never advance to `Position`/`AttackZone`. The wheel stays POLLED in `PlayerTick` (no new InputAction) and INERT outside a pick.
- **The select circle is a TRANSIENT pick visual and is DESTROYED at confirm.** Hold/Ambush transfer their circles to the group as persistent ground markers because those groups own a fixed piece of ground; a Follow group owns none, so a persistent marker would sit lying at a stale spot. `PositionMarkerDecal`/`AttackMarkerDecal` stay null for Follow.
- **`CancelGroupPick()` remains the ONE teardown call** and must cover the Follow stage at all 8 existing teardown sites (the melee-release-before-early-out law). Follow's pick is mutually exclusive with spell targeting and placement mode, exactly like the other two.

### 2. ⚠️ THE DEFAULT-STANCE LAW — FOLLOW IS THE SPAWN DEFAULT, AND NOTHING PLAYER-SIDE AUTO-ENGAGES ANY MORE
**This is a deliberate, Jonathan-confirmed change to the core game loop, not a side effect. Record it as such; do not let a later task "restore" auto-march as a bugfix.** Today a played Blue unit marches at the enemy castle unprompted. From this batch on:
- **Every FOLLOW-ELIGIBLE Blue unit auto-enrolls in the follow group at `BeginPlay`, on EVERY spawn path** — player placement (`SpawnUnitSwarm`), `ABarracks` spawner output, `SummonTestUnit` — because the enroll lives on the UNIT's `BeginPlay`, not on one controller call site. It walks to the hero and stays passive until the player gives it Attack / Defend / Hold / Ambush.
- **THERE IS EXACTLY ONE FOLLOW GROUP PER CONTROLLER.** `int32 DefaultFollowGroupId` (transient, `INDEX_NONE`), lazily created by `EnsureDefaultFollowGroup()`. Pressing **C** does NOT create a second follow group — it ADDS the circled eligible units to that same one (stealing them out of any Hold/Ambush group, per the existing steal law). This is what makes "the spawn default" and "the C command" the same object, and it is why Jonathan's "there is only one … circle used for this" is satisfiable.
- **Enrollment is UNCONDITIONAL and does not inherit the last order.** A unit spawned after the player pressed T still spawns FOLLOWING. **Accepted ergonomic consequence, FLAGGED:** reinforcements must be re-commanded each wave. If that plays as fiddly, the lever is a "new spawns inherit the last issued order" toggle — NOT a silent change to this law.
- **The release law is unchanged and now load-bearing:** **T** (Attack stance) and **E** (Defend stance) clear ALL groups, follow group included — T is therefore the "everyone attack" button. The follow group re-creates lazily on the next spawn or C press. Play Again clears it with everything else.
- **WHAT IS *NOT* COVERED, stated precisely so the pacing claim is honest:** (a) **Siege units — Ogre and Sapper — are NOT follow-eligible and are UNCHANGED: they still auto-march at the enemy castle.** "Nothing auto-engages" is true of the *commandable* player fleet, not of the whole board. (b) **Bot / Red units are entirely unchanged** — the opponent still plays the game by itself. (c) The LEGACY Standard body and `UpdateStateStandardCommanded` are **KEPT byte-identical**: they still serve Red, still serve Siege, and still serve any Blue unit whose group is released. Deleting them is not part of this batch.

### 4. THE FOLLOW BODY — dispatch, the hero anchor, and the never-attack guarantee
- **DISPATCH POINT (load-bearing):** `ASummonedUnit::UpdateState`'s order today is *freeze early-out → `TrackChargeMovement` → **Profile dispatch (Siege → `UpdateStateSiege`, Support → `UpdateStateSupport`, both `return`)** → group dispatch → stance gate → legacy body*. A Support Cleric therefore **returns before the group dispatch is ever reached**. **The Follow dispatch is HOISTED ABOVE the Profile dispatch** — if `CommandGroupId` resolves to a live group of type `Follow`, run `UpdateStateFollow(Group)` and return, whatever the profile. Hold/Ambush dispatch stays exactly where it is. This hoist is the ONE structural change to `UpdateState`, and every other branch stays byte-identical.
- **`UpdateStateFollow` NEVER ACQUIRES AND NEVER ATTACKS.** It force-nulls `CurrentTarget` and never calls `AcquireTarget` / `AcquireEnemyNearPoint` / `EnterAttack`. This is a per-BODY seal (a following Footman is still a normal attacker the moment it leaves the group), which is why it must not be conflated with `CanEverAttack()` — that one is per-CLASS and permanent.
- **THE HERO ANCHOR IS RESOLVED LIVE, EVERY STATE TICK, AND NEVER CACHED.** `ASiegePlayerController::GetFollowAnchor()` returns the owning controller's live pawn (the hero) or nullptr. The unit reaches it through **`ASiegePlayerController::FindControllerForTeam(World, Team)`** — the M8 owning-team resolve. **`GetFirstPlayerController()` stays BANNED in gameplay code** (M8 TEAM LAW). Resolving live is also what makes hero respawn work for free: the game mode's respawn path may hand back a **different pawn actor**, and a cached pointer would follow a corpse forever.
- **🚩 HERO-DEATH RULING (manager, Jonathan did not specify — DEFAULT SHIPS, FLAGGED):** while the anchor is null or the hero is dead, **following units HOLD POSITION** — `EnterIdle()`, no target, no attack, no march — and **resume following the instant a live hero pawn resolves again**, including a brand-new post-respawn pawn. Rejected alternatives, recorded so they are not re-derived: marching to the corpse (a conga line to a hidden body deep in enemy territory), and falling back to Defend (that would make them fight, breaking Jonathan's ruling that following units never attack). **Accepted consequence:** the hero respawns at `PlayerStart (−23800, 0, 100)`, so on resume a squad caught mid-field walks the full distance home. That is a pacing item for the playtest, not a defect.
- **⚠️ ANTI-REPATH IS A REQUIREMENT, NOT A POLISH ITEM.** The anchor MOVES. Re-issuing `EnterAdvanceToLocation` at a fresh station every 0.25 s state tick is precisely the re-path-every-tick mill that produced the TASK-280 / TASK-282 "units freeze just past midfield" and "halt just short of the castle" bugs. Law: **re-issue the move only when the recomputed station has drifted more than `FollowRepathTolerance` from the last issued goal**, and keep the existing 150 uu arrival tolerance for the idle test. A follow implementation that re-paths unconditionally is a QA FAIL.
- **Formation:** the per-unit station is `HeroLocation + GroupStationOffset`, with `GroupStationOffset` computed ONCE at enroll from a deterministic golden-angle sunflower inside `FollowFormationRadius` (the shipped squad-spread recipe, re-anchored on a moving point instead of a fixed circle). Per-unit scalars only — **no arrays on units** (the shipped law).
- **Replication posture (M8 DECLARATION DUTY): this batch adds NO replicated property and NO new replicated class**, so **no new relevancy tier is declared** — and that sentence must appear in the handoffs, because "tier not declared" is a QA FAIL and "there is nothing to declare" is only acceptable when it is stated. Follow-group state lives on `ASiegePlayerController` (owner-relevant, a player's private command surface) exactly like `UnitGroups` does today. In M8 P1 units are server-only, so the whole feature is play-verifiable **host/single-player only** until P2.

### 5. ⚠️ THE MINER COMMAND SEMANTICS TABLE — Jonathan's words are the spec

### 6. ⚠️ THE MINER SEAL — what may be re-opened, what may not, and the two candidate approaches
`AMinerUnit` is sealed **structurally** (class doc + `qa/TASK-021-report.md` WARN-1): `StateCheckInterval = 0` (so `SetTimer` with rate ≤ 0 CLEARS instead of scheduling — the base state machine's only driver never runs) · `AggroRadius = 0` · `ClearAllTimersForObject(this)` immediately after `Super::BeginPlay()`. **The seal exists because the Miner row carries `Cadence 0` and `LoadStatsAndStart` clamps to `MinAttackCadence` 0.05 s — a miner that ever reached the Attack state would swing 20×/s.** Commands need a decision loop, so this seal must be re-opened **without re-opening the ability to attack**.
- **NAMED PRECEDENT: `ASorcererUnit` (TASK-360).** It is commandable and cannot attack, and it achieves that by **keeping its state timer** and sealing at the chokepoints via `CanEverAttack() → false`, enforced at **THREE guard points in `ASummonedUnit.cpp`** — `EnterAttack()` (stands the unit down to Idle), `UpdateStateGrouped()` (acquires nothing), `PerformAttack()` (refuses). Those guards are ALREADY SHIPPED and already cover any class that returns false.
- **`AMinerUnit::CanEverAttack()` MUST return `false`, whichever approach is chosen.** It is free, it is already pinned, and it makes the three shipped guard points cover the miner permanently. This is not optional and is not the approach decision.
- **THE APPROACH IS THE PROGRAMMER'S, WITH A WRITTEN JUSTIFICATION — but here is the trap that decides it, measured from the shipped code, so nobody rediscovers it the hard way.** `UpdateState`'s profile dispatch (`SummonedUnit.cpp:1197-1211`) routes **Siege** and **Support** away, and lets **Standard *and* `None`** fall through to the legacy Standard body. **The Miner is `Profile == None`.** So the moment `StateCheckInterval` becomes non-zero, the miner starts running the **legacy Standard body** — which, with no target, sets `Goal = FindNearestEnemyCastle()` and issues `EnterAdvance`. **An unsealed miner marches on the enemy castle**, and `AggroRadius = 0` does not prevent it (that only blocks acquisition). Any approach that lifts the timer seal must handle this explicitly.
  - **(A) UNSEAL** — restore `StateCheckInterval`, drop the `ClearAllTimersForObject` sweep, add `CanEverAttack() → false`, and route `Profile == None` away from the legacy body. Maximum reuse of `UpdateStateFollow`/`UpdateStateGrouped`; but it re-opens the exact trap the seal exists for and risks **double-driving the walk** (the base's `EnterAdvance` fighting `EnsureWalkingToNode` for the same movement component).
  - **(B) KEEP THE SEAL, READ COMMANDS IN THE MINER'S OWN POLL** — `UpdateMining` already runs every `ArrivalCheckInterval` (0.25 s, the same cadence as the state timer). Resolve the owning-team controller + group there and steer the destination. Zero risk of a 20×/s attack, zero risk of a castle march, zero double-drive; costs a small duplication of the group resolve and the station-offset math.
  - **Manager's leaning is (B) or a hybrid** on blast-radius grounds, but **(A) with the fall-through handled is acceptable if the programmer's evidence supports it.** What is NOT acceptable is choosing silently: the handoff states the approach, the double-drive analysis, and the `Profile == None` fall-through disposition.
- **REGRESSION LAW for the miner (QA criteria, all of them):** an **un-commanded** miner is behaviorally identical to today (walk → register → +1 gold/s → wait-mode → eviction → all-depleted idle) · the §3.3 miner cap, the per-tenure income latches and the death bookkeeping are untouched · `AGoldNode`'s exclusive-occupancy claim is untouched · `FreezeAI` still kills every miner timer at match end · a miner **NEVER** enters Attack under ANY of the five commands, and QA cites the guard points by file:line.

### 9. Card-face ACCEPTANCE GATE — the tool name, reserved here before its task issues
