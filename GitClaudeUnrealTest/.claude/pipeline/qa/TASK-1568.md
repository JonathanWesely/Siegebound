- **The config, read:** `C:\Program Files\Git\etc\gitconfig` sets `core.autocrlf = true`. `.git\config` has no override, and there is no `.git\config.worktree` and no `.git\info\attributes`.

Its nine lines are LFS `-text` rules for binary types, and none matches `*.log` or `*.ps1`, so autocrlf governs these files and the handoff's conclusion stands (NIT-2).
- **The bytes, by my reading of git's behaviour (not run):**
  - On add, CRLF becomes LF in the blob.
  - On checkout, LF becomes CRLF.
  - So `cook-bp-green.log` (all CRLF) round-trips byte-identical.
  - `empty.log` stays 0 bytes.
  - `cook-bp-error-red.log` (11 CRLF + 2 lone LF) comes back **+2 B with a different sha** on any fresh checkout.
  - The LF-only `.ps1` files come back as CRLF on a fresh checkout. That was already true of `ship.ps1`.
  - Git warns on add for the LF and mixed files. This is the expected round-trip warning, not an error.
- **Does it matter to the harness? No.** The harness asserts verdicts, reasons and counts, never bytes. `Select-String` splits CRLF and LF alike, so the red hits stay on lines 3/4/6/7 and the green summary stays on line 11.
- **What it does matter to:** the red fixture's sha anchor, and the "byte-identical to source" claim. Both hold for the working tree at the commit instant, not for a later clone. That is WARN-4, for the manager or host.
