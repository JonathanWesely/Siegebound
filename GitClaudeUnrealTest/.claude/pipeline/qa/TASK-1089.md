### ⚖️ RULING 1 — the preflight: does it genuinely discriminate *available* from *gated*? **YES, at one rung — and the handoff claims one rung more than it earned.**

- **404 (route absent on this account)** — ruled out.
- **401 (key not accepted for this route)** — ruled out.
- **403 at the *route* level** — ruled out, and this is the real inference. A route-level authorization layer runs *before* body validation in essentially every middleware stack; a reply that came from the handler's own required-field check therefore proves no earlier layer refused. The second probe strengthens it materially: reaching **per-field enum validation** (`AIModel must be one of […]`) is deeper into the handler than the required-field check.

**Where the claim overreaches.** The handoff writes *"Not 404 (absent), not 403 (plan-gated)."* The second clause is true only of **route-level** gating. It does **not** rule out **create-time entitlement gating** — a plan check that runs *after* body validation, at enqueue. An empty body cannot reach that layer **by construction**: it never becomes a valid create. So the untested rung is not merely *"does a usable mesh come back"* (the author's stated limit) — it is one rung lower: ***does a VALID create get past entitlement at all.***

**And that is the most creditable thing in this diff:** the residual uncertainty the preflight cannot remove is *precisely* the case the exit-4 branch was written for — a 403/404 on the create is loud, verbatim, and explicitly says nothing fell back.
